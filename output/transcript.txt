 Today we will discuss the basic concepts of cyber security. Cyber security refers to the practice and technologies used to protect computer systems, networks, programs and data from unauthorized access, damage or cyber attacks. As individuals and organisations increasingly rely on digital technology, cyber security has become an important part of protecting sensitive information and maintaining reliable computer systems. One important concept in cyber security is the CIA TRIET. CIA stands for Confidentiality, Integrity and Availability. Confidentiality refers to ensuring that information can only be assessed by authorised users. For example, passwords and encryption can be used to prevent unauthorized individuals from assessing sensitive information. Integrity refers to protecting information from unauthorized modification. This helps ensure that data remains accurate and trustworthy. Availability refers to ensuring that the systems and information remain accessible and authorised users need them. Cyber security systems must protect users against several different types of threats. On-commentar it's malware. Malware is malicious software designed to damage computer systems, steal information or perform unwanted activities. Examples of malware include viruses, ransomware, spyware and computer worms. Ransomware is particularly harmful because it can equip a user's files and demand payment before assessed with a files is restored. Another common cyber security trick is phishing. Phishing attacks attempt to trick users into revealing sensitive information such as passwords or financial information. Attackers may send emails or messages that appear to come from legitimate organisations. These messages often contain malicious links or attempt to convince users to provide personal information. Users can reduce the risk of phishing attacks or carefully check suspicious messages and avoiding unknown links. Authentication is another important component of cyber security. Traditional authentication commonly relies on user names and passwords. However passwords can be stolen, guess or reuse across multiple services. Multi-factor authentication includes security by requiring users to provide more than one form of verification. For example, a user may enter a password and then confirm the identity using a temporary security code sent to another device. Encryption can also be used to protect sensitive information. Encryption converts readable information into an encoded format that cannot easily be understood without the appropriate encryption key. Encryption is commonly used when transmitting information across the internet and when storing sensitive data. Finally, cyber security is not only a technical problem. Human behaviour also plays an important role in maintaining security. Users should create strong passwords, keep software updated, avoid suspicious downloads and remain cautious when receiving unexpected messages. Organisations can also provide cyber security awareness training to help employees recognise common threats. In summary, cyber security involves protecting computer systems, networks, and information from digital threats. Important cyber security concepts include confidentiality, integrity, availability, malware protection, phishing awareness, authentication and encryption. Combining appropriate technologies with responsible user behaviour can help individuals and organisations reduce cyber security risk.
